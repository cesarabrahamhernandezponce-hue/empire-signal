serendipity
/ˌser.ənˈdɪp.ə.ti/
the occurrence of finding valuable things without looking for them
The Etymology
Coined in 1754 by Horace Walpole, in a letter describing an unexpected discovery. He took it from a Persian fairy tale, ‘The Three Princes of Serendip’ — princes who kept finding things they weren’t looking for.
The Deeper Layer
‘Serendip’ was the old Persian name for Sri Lanka — from Sanskrit ‘Simhaladvipa,’ meaning ‘Island of Lions.’ A word about lucky accidents was itself born from one.
